Why local matters

Masonry in these hills is a different job than masonry anywhere else.

Western Connecticut sits on glacial till. It is rocky, uneven, and full of ledge that shows up exactly where you planned to dig. Two properties on the same street can need completely different foundations. That is why we will not quote a job from a photo.

Connecticut's building code puts footings at 42 inches to get below the frost line. Plenty of walls and steps around here were set shallower than that, and every winter the ground lifts them a little further out of level. It is the most common reason we get called to fix somebody else's work.

The freeze-thaw cycle is relentless. Water gets into a hairline crack or an open mortar joint, freezes, expands, and opens it wider. Do that a few dozen times a winter and a sound wall becomes a leaning one. The defense is unglamorous: the right base, the right mortar, joints that shed water, and drainage that actually goes somewhere.

On the sloped lots common through Ridgefield, Redding and around Candlewood Lake, drainage is most of the job. Get it right and the wall lasts decades. Get it wrong and no amount of good stonework saves it.

Questions

What people ask us.

Do I need a permit for masonry work?

It depends on the town and the project. Retaining walls above a certain height, structural work and anything touching a foundation usually need one; a patio at grade often does not. We tell you at the estimate, and we pull the permit ourselves through your town's building department.

How much does the work cost?

Every property here is different. Slope, soil, access and how much ledge is in the ground all move the number. That is why the estimate is free and on-site. You get a written price with the full scope before anything starts, and it does not change unless you change the scope.

How long does a project take?

Most steps and walkways are done in under a week. Patios and walls typically run one to three weeks depending on size and site access. Chimney repairs are often a day or two. We give you real dates at the estimate.

Do you work in the winter?

Yes. Chimney work, interior fireplaces and general contracting run year-round. Some outdoor masonry needs stable temperatures to cure properly, so we will tell you honestly whether your project should wait for spring rather than rush it and have it fail.

Why is my patio or walkway cracking and lifting?

Almost always the base. If it was not excavated deep enough, compacted properly, or drained, water sits underneath, freezes, and pushes the surface up. Once it starts, resetting the top does not fix it. The base has to be rebuilt. We would rather tell you that at the estimate than after.

Are you licensed and insured?

Yes. Connecticut Home Improvement Contractor registration #0631958, and we carry general liability and workers' compensation. A certificate of insurance is available on request.

Who actually does the work?

Nedzat and the crew. The owner is on the job, not just at the estimate. That has been the arrangement since 1999, and it is the reason the reviews read the way they do.
