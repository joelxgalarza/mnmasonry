Masonry Contractor in Redding, CT

Redding is heavily wooded, sparsely built and made up of large lots reached by long driveways. Most properties here are on well and septic rather than public utilities, and that single fact shapes how excavation on a Redding job has to be planned.

It is also one of the best towns in the region for genuinely old stone.

How we work here

We locate the septic system before we break ground

On a property with public sewer, excavation planning is mostly about avoiding utilities. On a Redding property it also means avoiding the septic tank, the distribution box and, most importantly, the leach field.

Driving a machine across a leach field can crush the distribution lines, and building a patio, wall or driveway over one is worse, because it seals the field and permanently damages a system that costs many times more than the masonry to replace.

So we establish where the system is before anything is designed, from the town's records, the as built plan if the homeowner has it, or by locating it on site. Occasionally it moves a patio ten feet. That is a much better outcome than the alternative.

Redding specifics

Long driveways and old walls

Redding driveways are frequently several hundred feet of gravel through trees, which affects delivery, spoil removal and what size vehicle can turn around at the top. Material staging has to be planned rather than assumed, and it is worth knowing before a stone delivery arrives at the bottom of the drive.

The compensation is the stone. Redding has an unusual concentration of eighteenth and nineteenth century farm walls running through its woodland, much of it on private property and much of it collapsed. Rebuilding those, or reusing that stone in new work, produces results that no delivered pallet can match.

Questions

What Redding homeowners ask us.

Will you damage my septic system?

Not if we know where it is, which is why locating it is the first thing we do. We plan access routes and the position of any new masonry around the tank and leach field.

Can you reuse the old stone walls on my land?

Usually yes, and it is worth asking about. Weathered local stone with lichen on it produces a result you cannot buy, and it often costs less than trucking in new material.

Is access a problem on a long private drive?

It is a planning question rather than a problem. We check turning space, overhead clearance and surface condition at the estimate so deliveries are scheduled around what the driveway can actually take.
